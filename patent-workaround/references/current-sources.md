# Current-law, claim-scope, and prior-art sources

Refresh these sources on every run. Record the access date, displayed revision or
effective date, controlling application dates, and later guidance or precedent. Do
not treat the links in this file as proof that a source remains current.

## Source hierarchy

1. **Statutes**: current Title 35 from the U.S. House Office of the Law Revision
   Counsel, especially sections 100-103, 111-123, 132, 251, 253, 271, and 282:
   <https://uscode.house.gov/browse/prelim@title35&edition=prelim>.
2. **Rules**: current 37 CFR Part 1 in eCFR, especially rules governing claims,
   amendments, new matter, support statements, and prosecution:
   <https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-37/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-1>.
3. **Binding law**: current U.S. Supreme Court and Federal Circuit opinions. Use
   official court opinions or PDFs where available and verify later treatment. For
   scope questions, refresh claim construction, all-elements, doctrine of
   equivalents, prosecution-history estoppel/disclaimer, disclosure-dedication,
   divided infringement, inducement, contributory infringement, territoriality,
   and prior-art-ensnarement law as the facts require.
4. **USPTO procedure**: current MPEP and later examination memoranda. The MPEP
   describes Office practice and is not itself substantive law.
5. **Primary technical evidence and prior art**: patents/publications, standards,
   papers, manuals, source material, archives, and product evidence with verified
   public-availability dates.

## Claim-scope research discipline

Infringement-related doctrine falls outside ordinary USPTO examination. Do not rely
on an MPEP summary as the controlling source for infringement scope. Locate the
current binding opinion, verify later treatment, and apply it only to established
facts. At minimum, research when relevant:

- claim construction and the role of specification and prosecution history;
- literal all-elements analysis;
- 35 U.S.C. 112(f) scope;
- doctrine of equivalents and its limitation-by-limitation application;
- prosecution-history estoppel and disclaimer;
- disclosure-dedication and prior-art ensnarement;
- divided or indirect infringement and actor attribution;
- territorial and importation limits.

Do not convert a screening claim chart into a legal opinion.

## Prior-art sources

Use broad discovery tools, then verify and preserve the actual primary reference:

- USPTO Patent Public Search: <https://ppubs.uspto.gov/pubwebapp/>
- USPTO Patent Center: <https://patentcenter.uspto.gov/applications/search>
- WIPO PATENTSCOPE: <https://patentscope.wipo.int/search/en/search.jsf>
- EPO Espacenet: <https://worldwide.espacenet.com/>
- NPL from publishers, standards bodies, repositories, archives, product manuals,
  source repositories, regulatory records, and other date-verifiable sources.

Google Patents and ordinary web search may discover references, but verify identity,
family, dates, and relied-on disclosure using the patent publication or an official
database. Preserve PDFs or stable full-text copies.

Treat every unverified aggregator or search result as a discovery lead only. Do not
use it to declare a claim anticipated, obvious, or unsafe to pursue. If the official
bibliographic record, full relied-on text, and relevant dates cannot be verified,
mark the patentability gate `cannot-assess`, identify the lead, and state what remains
to be checked.

Search the existing claim as a whole and each proposed coverage expansion: omitted
limitations, substitute genera, actor boundaries, new claim classes, functional
relationships, and the combination that preserves the value proposition. Search
commercial products and standards as well as patent terminology.
