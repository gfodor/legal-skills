# Part I — Filing packet and formalities

48 items (17 Fatal).

Grounded in 35 U.S.C. §§ 111, 115, 119, 122; 37 CFR 1.6–1.10, 1.16–1.33, 1.51–1.78, 1.97–1.98, 1.213; MPEP 502–609. Fee amounts, form numbers, and EFS/Patent Center mechanics must be re-verified against the current USPTO fee schedule and forms page before filing.

## Specification formatting

### `I01` Verify uniform sheet size and 1-inch margins on every sheet
- **Check:** Every typed sheet of the specification, claims, and abstract is the same size (U.S. letter or A4), single-sided, and carries at least 2.5 cm (1") top, bottom, left, and right margins.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Mixed letter/A4 in the text packet. Measure the top margin above the page numbers. Nothing on the reverse. If foreign filing is contemplated, confirm a photocopy onto A4 still has legal A4 margins.
- **Why it matters:** Nonconforming papers draw an OPAP objection. A packet the Office will not accept as complete is treated as deposited, not filed, which puts the filing date at risk.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.52; MPEP 608.01.

### `I02` Verify line spacing, typeface, and sheet numbering
- **Check:** The text is 1.5- or double-spaced, set in a conventional readable face of at least 12 point, unjustified, with sheets numbered top or bottom, centered, inside the margin.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Single-spaced claims or tables pasted in from another document. Type smaller than 12 point in footnotes or captions. Page numbers that sit in the margin. The same spacing rule applies to later correspondence.
- **Why it matters:** Improperly formatted papers invite a Notice to File Corrected Application Papers before examination starts.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.52; MPEP 608.01.

### `I03` Verify claims and abstract start on new pages, with the abstract last
- **Check:** The claims begin on a fresh page, each claim is separated from the next by an extra line, the abstract begins on its own page and is the last sheet, and the title appears on the first page.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Claims running on after the last description paragraph. An abstract placed up front in published-patent style.
- **Why it matters:** Misordered parts draw formal objections and complicate the excess-claim fee count.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.52, 1.72, 1.75; MPEP 608.01(a)–(b).

### `I04` Verify the draft is not formatted to imitate an issued patent, and consider paragraph numbering
- **Check:** The application is laid out as a double-spaced typed document, not as two narrow single-spaced columns with a references-cited list, and non-heading paragraphs are numbered [0001], [0002] in brackets.
- **Severity:** Quality
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Reverse-engineered granted-patent PDFs. The Office adds front-page data at issuance. Bracketed paragraph numbers make later amendments replace whole paragraphs cleanly.
- **Why it matters:** A patent-mimicking layout is a formal defect. Missing paragraph numbers make every later amendment harder to enter.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.52, 1.121; MPEP 608.01, 714.

### `I05` Verify drawing sheets are all present and show every claimed feature
- **Check:** Every drawing sheet referenced in the specification is in the packet, the drawings depict each feature recited in the claims, and each sheet is numbered n/total below the top margin.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Cross-read claims against figures, then the brief description against the actual sheets. Preserve CAD sources. Confirm numerals match the specification both ways.
- **Why it matters:** Drawings that omit a claimed feature draw a § 112 objection. Missing sheets produce a Notice to File Missing Parts.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.83–1.84; 35 U.S.C. § 113.

## Inventorship, declaration, and signatures

### `I06` Verify that all true inventors, and only true inventors, are named
- **Check:** Every named person contributed to the conception of at least one claim, and no financier, employer, supervisor, or assistant has been added.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** The inventor list on page one, the ADS, and the declaration must match exactly, including spelling. Test each name against a claim limitation. Joint inventors need not have worked together or contributed equally.
- **Why it matters:** Misjoinder or nonjoinder can invalidate the patent. Naming a non-inventor is a false statement under oath.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. §§ 115–116; 37 CFR 1.41, 1.63; MPEP 602.

### `I07` Verify the declaration is complete, signed, dated, and consistent with the ADS
- **Check:** A declaration on the correct form variant is present, signed and dated by each inventor, with the title and inventor names identical to those on the ADS.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** PTO/AIA/01 when an ADS accompanies the filing; otherwise PTO/AIA/08 plus AIA/10 supplemental sheets. Confirm current form versions on the USPTO forms page. Each inventor must actually have read the specification. Retain the ink original if a scan is filed.
- **Why it matters:** A missing or defective declaration produces a Notice to File Missing Parts and a surcharge. A knowingly false declaration can invalidate the patent and expose the signer to perjury.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 115; 37 CFR 1.63; MPEP 602.

### `I08` Verify no changes were made to the application after the declaration was signed
- **Check:** The application text is byte-identical to what existed when the declaration was signed, and any handwritten correction is in ink, dated, and initialed in the adjacent margin *before* the signature date.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Compare the signature date against the document's last-modified date. If more than trivial changes are needed, execute a fresh declaration after the changes, or file a post-filing amendment that adds no new matter.
- **Why it matters:** A declaration signed before the application was complete can cause the application to be stricken or rejected.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.52(c), 1.63; MPEP 602.01.

## Application Data Sheet

### `I09` Verify the ADS is present and its optional sections are correctly resolved
- **Check:** Form PTO/AIA/14 is in the packet, "Filing by Reference" is left blank unless actually used, the AIA transition statement box is resolved deliberately, and the access opt-out boxes reflect the applicant's actual intent.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** The Office extracts bibliographic data from the ADS. A wrongly checked transition or access box is a substantive misstatement. Title, inventor names, and correspondence data must match the declaration and transmittal.
- **Why it matters:** Bad ADS data propagates into the filing receipt and the published application.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.76; MPEP 601.05.

### `I10` Verify the ADS is signed by a party authorized to sign it
- **Check:** The ADS signature matches one of the permitted scenarios, and in particular a registered practitioner has signed if any applicant is a juristic entity.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Read the current signature rules on the form. An inventor signature will not do for a corporate applicant. An unsigned ADS, or one signed by only the first of several joint inventors, fails.
- **Why it matters:** An improperly signed ADS is not effective, so benefit claims and a nonpublication request on it may not be given effect.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.33, 1.76; MPEP 605.

### `I11` Verify any provisional benefit claim appears in both the specification and the ADS
- **Check:** If a provisional is being relied on, its serial number and filing date appear in the CROSS-REFERENCE section *and* in the ADS, and the application is being filed within one year of the provisional.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** applications claiming PPA benefit
- **What to look for:** Placeholders instead of a real serial number. A claim in only one of the two required places. The transmittal has no box for this — do not expect to find it there. At least one inventor must be named on both applications.
- **Why it matters:** A benefit claim not properly made loses the earlier date. Filing after the anniversary loses it entirely.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 119(e); 37 CFR 1.78; MPEP 211.02.

## Fees and entity status

### `I12` Verify small-entity status is actually earned before it is asserted
- **Check:** If small-entity status is claimed, the invention has not been assigned or licensed — and there is no obligation to assign or license it — to an entity that does not itself qualify.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** small entity filings
- **What to look for:** Employment, consulting, joint-development, or funding agreements that create an *obligation* to assign. Status must be consistent on the transmittal, fee calculation, and EFS/Patent Center page. Re-read current 37 CFR 1.27 — headcount and related-entity rules move.
- **Why it matters:** Asserting small-entity status without entitlement is a false certification and an underpayment.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.27; 35 U.S.C. § 41; current USPTO entity-status guidance.

### `I13` Verify micro-entity status against every prong, and that the certification is attached
- **Check:** If micro-entity status is claimed, each prong of the definition is satisfied and the current certification form is included.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** micro entity filings
- **What to look for:** Prior-application count, income limit, and no assignment/obligation to a non-micro entity — applied to each inventor. The college-employee alternative route. Form SB/15A or 15B (or current successor) must actually be in the packet. Re-check entitlement at every later fee payment.
- **Why it matters:** An unsupported micro-entity certification is a large underpayment and a false certification.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.29; current USPTO micro-entity page.

### `I14` Verify the claim count, page count, and filing mode against the fee calculation
- **Check:** Total claims, independent claims, multiple dependent claims, application page count, and paper-versus-electronic filing are all counted correctly and reflected in the fees being paid.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Count claims yourself. Flag any multiple dependent. Compute extras over 20 total and over 3 independent. Check the 100-sheet size fee. Paper utility filings add 37 CFR 1.16(t). Re-price against the current schedule — do not use a stale table.
- **Why it matters:** Underpayment produces a Notice to File Missing Parts and a surcharge.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.16; current USPTO fee schedule.

### `I15` Verify the payment instrument is complete and that all three basic fees are paid together
- **Check:** The basic filing, search, and examination fees are being paid simultaneously, and the payment method is complete, signed, and funded.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications (payment form varies by filing mode)
- **What to look for:** Paying only the "filing fee." Incomplete credit-card forms. PIN-required debit cards the Office will not accept. Confirm current accepted payment methods on uspto.gov.
- **Why it matters:** A rejected payment triggers a surcharge. An incomplete fee means the application is deficient rather than filed.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.16, 1.22–1.23; MPEP 509.

## Information Disclosure Statement and the duty of candor

### `I16` Verify the IDS is filed with the application or calendared within three months
- **Check:** If any inventor is aware of relevant prior art, an IDS either accompanies the packet or a diarised deadline exists to file it within three months of filing (or before the first Office Action).
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** all applications where prior art is known
- **What to look for:** A search or personal knowledge of art means an IDS is owed (37 CFR 1.97). If genuinely no art is known, keep a written memorandum to that effect. Do not complete a certificate of mailing on an IDS filed with the application.
- **Why it matters:** Failing to disclose known material art can render the patent unenforceable — even if the examiner independently finds the withheld reference.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.56, 1.97; MPEP 609, 2001; *Therasense*.

### `I17` Verify the IDS lists every known material reference on the proper form
- **Check:** Every prior-art reference known to any inventor or assignee is listed on the current IDS form, including references already discussed in the specification.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** all applications where prior art is known
- **What to look for:** Narrative discussion in the Background is not a substitute for SB/08a–b (or current successors). Include inventors' own papers and foreign-search hits. When in doubt, cite.
- **Why it matters:** Withholding a known material reference supports inequitable-conduct unenforceability.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.56, 1.98; MPEP 609.

### `I18` Verify copies of non-U.S. references and an explanation of non-English references are attached
- **Check:** The packet includes a copy of every cited foreign patent and every NPL item, plus a concise explanation of the relevance of any non-English-language reference.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications citing non-U.S. or non-patent prior art
- **What to look for:** U.S. patents and PGPubs need no copies. Foreign patents and NPL do. Rule 98(a)(3) requires a concise explanation of non-English items — without volunteering that a reference teaches a claimed feature. Use two-letter country codes.
- **Why it matters:** An IDS missing required copies or the Rule 98 explanation may not be considered, so the duty is not discharged.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.98; MPEP 609.

### `I19` Verify the IDS is not padded, and that copies are clean
- **Check:** The reference list contains only genuinely relevant, non-duplicative items, and every submitted copy is free of handwritten marks and notes.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications filing an IDS
- **What to look for:** Under-citation and burial are both deceptive-intent risks. A very long list where a handful of references are clearly closest, plus highlighter and marginalia on the PDFs, fails.
- **Why it matters:** Burying relevant references among irrelevant ones can be held to be an attempt to deceive the Office.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.56; MPEP 2001.04; *Therasense*.

### `I20` Verify material information other than prior art has been disclosed
- **Check:** Any known relevant litigation, rival inventorship assertion, or pre-filing sale of a product embodying the invention is disclosed by narrative statement.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** The duty of candor is not limited to patents and publications. A tidy SB/08 list and no narrative, in a matter where the inventor has already been selling the product, is the tell. Explain why the information does not negate patentability.
- **Why it matters:** Undisclosed material facts support inequitable-conduct unenforceability.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.56; MPEP 2001.01.

## Nonpublication request and foreign filing

### `I21` Verify a Nonpublication Request is not being filed if foreign filing is possible
- **Check:** An NPR is present only where the applicant has decided *not* to file a corresponding application abroad or under an international agreement requiring 18-month publication.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** applications including an NPR
- **What to look for:** Uncertainty is disqualifying. An NPR next to PCT plans, an A4 specification prepared for foreign filing, or a Paris deadline in the file is a contradiction. If the ADS checkbox is used, a separate NPR form is unnecessary.
- **Why it matters:** A false NPR certification is a misstatement. Later foreign filing without timely rescission abandons the U.S. application.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 122(b); 37 CFR 1.213; MPEP 1122.

### `I22` Verify the NPR is filed with the application and the 45-day rescission duty is recorded
- **Check:** Any NPR is in the packet at filing (not planned for later), is properly signed, and the file records the 45-day rescission obligation that attaches if the application is later filed abroad.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** applications including an NPR
- **What to look for:** An NPR filed after the application is worthless. Use PTO/SB/36 to rescind. No NPR means electronic publication at 18 months and a public file.
- **Why it matters:** A late NPR is ineffective. Failing to rescind within 45 days of a foreign filing abandons the U.S. case.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.213; MPEP 1122.

## Electronic filing packet

### `I23` Verify the electronic packet contains the right parts, in the right formats
- **Check:** The Patent Center / EFS packet consists of the drawings, specification/claims/abstract, declaration, and ADS — with paper-only parts omitted — and every PDF meets current resolution and white-background requirements.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** EFS-Web filing
- **What to look for:** Confirm current Patent Center document codes and PDF rules (commonly 300 DPI, white background). DOCX may be permitted for text; drawings remain PDF. "/inventor name/" is an accepted signature convention. Upload each part as a separate document.
- **Why it matters:** Wrong formats cause server rejections that can delay a filing past a critical date.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.6; current Patent Center / EFS-Web legal framework documentation; MPEP 502.05.

### `I24` Verify the uploaded documents validate and the Acknowledgment Receipt is complete
- **Check:** The review page shows all required application parts with no fatal errors, the correct entity size was selected at fee calculation, and the printed Acknowledgment Receipt lists everything that was filed.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** EFS-Web filing
- **What to look for:** Red-triangle fatal errors (fonts, gray scans, unsupported PDF versions). Entity size matching the papers. The receipt is the only contemporaneous proof of what was filed.
- **Why it matters:** A part that failed to upload is not in the application.
- **Primary source:** MPEP 502.05; current Patent Center help.

## Paper filing mechanics and receipt

### `I25` Verify the transmittal and fee transmittal are complete and signed
- **Check:** Every applicable box on the current application transmittal is checked with matching counts, and both the transmittal and the fee transmittal are signed and dated.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** paper filing
- **What to look for:** Reconcile the transmittal against the envelope contents: page counts, drawing sheets, declaration, ADS, assignment, IDS, postcard, NPR. Do not use a miscellaneous box to refer to a provisional.
- **Why it matters:** Clerical staff work from the transmittal. A mismatch produces missing-parts correspondence.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.51; MPEP 601; current PTO/AIA/15.

### `I26` Verify a self-addressed, stamped receipt postcard listing every enclosed paper
- **Check:** A self-addressed, postage-paid postcard is enclosed whose back itemises every paper in the packet and leaves room for the Office date and serial-number sticker.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** paper filing
- **What to look for:** A card that says only "patent application enclosed" is nearly useless. List inventors, title, drawing sheets, specification pages, declaration, transmittals, payment, NPR, and IDS.
- **Why it matters:** The stamped postcard is the applicant's only near-term proof of what arrived and when. The Office occasionally loses mail.
- **Primary source:** MPEP 503; 37 CFR 1.6.

### `I27` Verify the mailing method is Priority Mail Express, not a prohibited alternative
- **Check:** The packet is going by "Priority Mail Express Post Office to Addressee" with the PME number entered on the transmittal, and no certificate of mailing or private courier is being used to obtain a mailing-date filing date.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** paper filing
- **What to look for:** 37 CFR 1.10 is the mailing-date rule. Registered mail, certified mail, fax, FedEx, and a certificate of mailing do not give a mailing-date filing date for an application. First-class mail is permitted but a lost package means no date at all. Use the current Mail Stop Patent Application address.
- **Why it matters:** Only PME (under § 1.10) gives a filing date equal to the mailing date. A lost first-class package can blow a provisional anniversary or a statutory bar.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.10; MPEP 512–513.

### `I28` Verify assembly order, retained copies, and an independent second reading
- **Check:** The papers are assembled in the prescribed order and fastened correctly, a complete copy is retained, and someone other than the drafter has checked the packet against the rules.
- **Severity:** Quality
- **Applies to:** paper filing (retention and second reading apply to all)
- **What to look for:** Specification/claims/abstract/declarations stapled; drawings clipped, not stapled. File one copy; keep the signed original. Joint inventors should also have signed a joint-owners agreement — kept by each inventor, not filed.
- **Why it matters:** A deficient packet is treated as deposited rather than filed. A lost original with no retained copy is unrecoverable.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.51–1.52; MPEP 601.

## Assignments

### `I29` Verify any assignment being recorded has a complete cover sheet and the recordation fee
- **Check:** If ownership is being transferred, the signed assignment is accompanied by a completed Recordation Form Cover Sheet, the recordation fee, and — where filed with the application — the corresponding transmittal entries.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** applications with an assignment
- **What to look for:** Assignment moves ownership, not inventorship. Cover sheet (PTO-1595): conveying parties, receiving party, conveyance type, execution date, application identification. Recording after the filing receipt arrives, when the serial number is known, is often cleaner.
- **Why it matters:** Recording without the fee or a complete cover sheet means the assignment is not recorded.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 261; 37 CFR Part 3; MPEP 302.

## Petitions to Make Special and prioritized examination

### `I30` Verify any Petition to Make Special rests on a currently permitted ground
- **Check:** A PTMS asserts only a currently recognized ground (for example age 65+, poor health, or other grounds still listed in MPEP 708.02) and is accompanied by the required evidence.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** applications including a Petition to Make Special
- **What to look for:** Grounds come and go. Do not rely on a stale five-ground list. Age-based petitions often use PTO/SB/130. Other grounds need a supporting declaration with detailed facts. Confirm the current MPEP 708.02 list and forms.
- **Why it matters:** A petition on an unqualified ground is dismissed. A bare petition without required evidence will not be granted.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.102; MPEP 708.02; current USPTO petition forms.

### `I31` Verify any Accelerated Examination or Track One request meets its structural requirements
- **Check:** An Accelerated Examination petition meets current claim-count, search, and IDS requirements; a Track One request carries the current prioritized-examination fee and claim limits.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** applications requesting accelerated or prioritized examination
- **What to look for:** Hard claim limits (commonly three independent / twenty total — re-verify). Accelerated Examination still demands a preexamination search and restrictive statements that can later be used against the patent. Track One is a paid alternative with fewer admissions. Weigh whether the admissions are worth making.
- **Why it matters:** The petition fails if limits or content requirements are unmet. Admissions in an accelerated-examination search can support inequitable-conduct attacks if art was missed.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.102(e); MPEP 708.02(a)–(b); current Track One fee schedule.

## Design applications

### `I32` Verify the design application packet contains all required parts
- **Check:** A design filing includes the required specification elements, black-line drawings, a declaration, an ADS, and payment (plus paper-only transmittal parts if not filed electronically).
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** design
- **What to look for:** Preamble stating nature and intended use; cross-reference; federal-research statement; figure descriptions; a single claim. Mark inapplicable sections "Not Applicable." Electronic packets drop paper-only transmittals.
- **Why it matters:** A design application missing a required element draws a missing-parts notice. The whole substance rests on the drawings and the single claim.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.151–1.154; MPEP 1503.

### `I33` Verify design drawings show only the exterior, with shading, and enough views
- **Check:** The design drawings show only external appearance, use no reference numerals, are executed with surface and edge shading, and include enough figures to disclose every detail of the external surface.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** design
- **What to look for:** Interior parts, hidden mechanisms, or cross-sections. Any reference numeral. Unshaded flat outlines. A missing rear or bottom face. Computer-generated icons must be shown on an article of manufacture (a display) in solid lines.
- **Why it matters:** Incomplete surface disclosure can invalidate the design patent. A symbol shown alone is not an article of manufacture under 35 U.S.C. § 171.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 171; 37 CFR 1.152; MPEP 1503.02, 1504.01(a).

### `I34` Verify the design application has one claim, one embodiment, and an IDS plan
- **Check:** The application recites exactly one claim in the prescribed form, is directed to a single inventive concept, and has an IDS calendared within three months if any prior art is known.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** design
- **What to look for:** "I claim the ornamental design for [title] as shown." Add "and described" if the specification elaborates beyond view-type sentences. Multiple versions invite restriction. Apply the same IDS discipline as utility.
- **Why it matters:** More than one claim or an unsupported claim form draws an objection. Missing the design IDS deadline carries the same candor exposure as in a utility case.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.153–1.154; 37 CFR 1.56, 1.97; MPEP 1503.01.

## Deadline register and packet completeness

### `I35` Recompute and calendar the provisional-to-nonprovisional one-year conversion date
- **Check:** If the draft claims benefit of a provisional, verify that the regular application is going out within one year of the provisional's filing date and that the same one-year date is calendared for foreign Convention filings.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** Applications claiming benefit of a Provisional Patent Application
- **What to look for:** Use the filing-receipt date. Confirm the benefit claim is on the ADS. Confirm inventor overlap and that the provisional supports at least one claim. The 20-year term runs from the earliest nonprovisional, so a provisional year does not shorten term. Foreign offices may not honor a U.S. weekend rollover.
- **Why it matters:** Missing the year destroys the priority claim. Any intervening art then applies against the later date.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 119(e); 37 CFR 1.7, 1.78.

### `I36` Confirm the provisional's disclosure actually supports the claims being filed
- **Check:** Verify that everything the regular application claims priority for is described in the provisional in enabling, best-mode terms — not merely mentioned.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** Applications claiming benefit of a Provisional Patent Application
- **What to look for:** A claim-by-claim support map. Broadened genera, new ranges, and new species added after the provisional. A marketing write-up or figure-only provisional is a classic worthless priority document. Keep the map in the file.
- **Why it matters:** An unsupported claim silently loses the provisional date even though the benefit claim is on file.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. §§ 112(a), 119(e); *Dynamic Drinkware*; MPEP 211.05.

### `I37` Establish that no pre-filing public disclosure has already foreclosed rights
- **Check:** Verify, on the record, whether the invention was published, offered for sale, sold, or publicly used before the intended filing date, and by whom.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** All applications
- **What to look for:** Documentary dates, not recollection. Under AIA, only inventor-originated disclosures within one year are excepted. An independent third-party disclosure is an outright bar. Demos, crowdfunding pages, and papers are the usual hidden events.
- **Why it matters:** A disclosure that predates the filing date, or a lapsed grace period, is an unfixable bar.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 102(a)–(b); MPEP 2152–2153.

### `I38` Protect foreign-filing rights against pre-filing sale or disclosure
- **Check:** Confirm that no sale or public disclosure of invention details has occurred before the U.S. filing date, and that any non-Convention filings are planned before the invention becomes publicly known.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** All applications with any foreign filing intent
- **What to look for:** Paris rights are stricter than U.S. rights: there is no grace period. A single pre-filing sale can forfeit them even where U.S. rights survive. Non-Convention novelty is destroyed by publicity, not by the U.S. date.
- **Why it matters:** Foreign rights forfeited by a pre-filing disclosure cannot be revived.
- **Primary source:** Paris Convention Art. 4; national absolute-novelty statutes.

### `I39` Calendar the Convention year and the PCT dates from the U.S. filing date
- **Check:** Verify that the 1-year Convention date, the 30-month national-stage date, and any Chapter II date are computed from the correct priority date and entered in the docket at filing.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** Applications with foreign filing intent
- **What to look for:** A single wrong seed date corrupts the chain. Verify against the filing receipt. Design Convention term is six months, not twelve. Chapter II has alternative triggers — revisit when the search report issues.
- **Why it matters:** Missing the Convention year loses priority abroad. Missing 30 months loses national-stage entry. Neither is curable in most jurisdictions.
- **Primary source:** Paris Art. 4; PCT Arts. 22, 39; 35 U.S.C. § 172.

### `I40` Verify the foreign-filing license or the 6-month wait
- **Check:** Confirm that no foreign filing is scheduled within 6 months of the U.S. filing date unless a foreign-filing license has been granted on the filing receipt.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** Applications with foreign filing intent
- **What to look for:** The license appears on the filing receipt, which does not exist at the moment of filing. Produce a docket instruction to check the receipt. Gate any fast PCT or foreign-counsel filing on the license.
- **Why it matters:** Foreign filing without a license and before six months can jeopardize the U.S. patent (35 U.S.C. §§ 184–186).
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. §§ 184–186; 37 CFR 5.11–5.15.

### `I41` Fix the 18-month publication date and the Nonpublication Request position
- **Check:** Confirm the 18-month publication date is docketed and that the decision to file or omit a Nonpublication Request is made and documented before the packet goes out.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** All applications; critical where foreign filing is contemplated
- **What to look for:** An NPR only works if filed with the application. Filing an NPR and then foreign-filing without rescinding it, or omitting an NPR when secrecy was wanted, are the two traps. If an NPR is filed, docket a rescission checkpoint tied to the Convention-year decision.
- **Why it matters:** Involuntary publication discloses the invention worldwide. Forgetting to rescind an NPR before foreign filing abandons the U.S. application.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 122(b); 37 CFR 1.211, 1.213.

### `I42` Apply the weekend and holiday rollover rule to every computed date
- **Check:** Verify that each calendared date has been tested against PTO closure days and rolled forward where required.
- **Severity:** Quality
- **Applies to:** All applications
- **What to look for:** 37 CFR 1.7. Spot-check anniversaries against weekends and the listed federal holidays observed in Washington, DC. Work to the unrolled date and treat rollover as margin. Do not assume a foreign office will honor a U.S. rollover.
- **Why it matters:** A date computed one business day wrong on a nonextendable period causes abandonment or loss of priority.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.7; MPEP 710.

### `I43` Verify the packet contains every required paper and a correctly computed fee set
- **Check:** Confirm the packet holds the transmittal (if paper), ADS, declaration, specification, claims, drawings, and fee payment, and that the fee total covers filing, search, examination, excess claims, multiple dependents, and any size fee at the correct entity rate.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** All applications; entity rate matters for small/micro entity
- **What to look for:** Count independents against three and total against twenty. Flag multiple dependents. Count sheets against 100. Entity status on the fee sheet must match the ADS and any assignment. Re-price against the current schedule. Paper utility filings add 1.16(t) on top of the basic fee.
- **Why it matters:** Underpayment triggers a surcharge and a missing-parts notice. A packet filed without the fee, oath, or at least one claim draws 37 CFR 1.16(f).
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.16, 1.51, 1.53; current USPTO fee schedule.

### `I44` Confirm the declaration form matches the ADS decision and covers every inventor
- **Check:** Verify that the packet uses the declaration variant appropriate to whether an ADS is being filed, and that every named inventor is captured, using the supplemental sheet if needed.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** All applications
- **What to look for:** AIA/01 with an ADS; AIA/08 without. Cross-check the inventor list against the ADS and any contribution statement. Attach AIA/10 rather than crowding or dropping a name. Every inventor signed and dated.
- **Why it matters:** A missing or defective oath draws the 1.16(f) surcharge. Wrong inventorship is a validity defect that follows the patent.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 115; 37 CFR 1.63, 1.76; current USPTO forms page.

### `I45` Check that every PTO form in the packet is a current, post-AIA version
- **Check:** Verify the expiration date printed at the top of each PTO form and confirm the packet uses the form set for applications filed on or after September 16, 2012.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** "Approved for use through" dates. Pre-AIA declarations or ADS forms pulled from an old template folder. Prefer current AIA-series numbers downloaded the week of filing. An expired form is not acceptable even if a printed book still shows it.
- **Why it matters:** The Office will not accept an expired form, which converts a filing into a missing-parts situation and can cost the intended date.
- **Primary source:** USPTO forms page (uspto.gov/patent/forms); 37 CFR 1.4.

### `I46` Resolve the optional-form decisions before the packet closes
- **Check:** Confirm a conscious, documented decision on each conditional form — Nonpublication Request, IDS, Petition to Make Special, credit-card form, and assignment plus recordation cover sheet.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** All applications; foreign filing intent drives the NPR decision
- **What to look for:** Inconsistency: an NPR in a packet whose file also contains a foreign-filing plan; an IDS cover letter with no SB/08; an executed assignment with no PTO-1595. Carry search hits onto the IDS. Route assignments to the assignment mail stop if filed on paper.
- **Why it matters:** An NPR filed against a foreign plan causes abandonment when it is not rescinded. A missing IDS can support an inequitable-conduct attack.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.56, 1.97, 1.213; 37 CFR Part 3.

### `I47` Verify the physical and editorial hygiene of the assembled papers
- **Check:** Confirm the papers are properly formatted for PTO submission and that no template artifacts remain in any downloaded form.
- **Severity:** Quality
- **Applies to:** All applications, especially paper filings and forms edited from USPTO templates
- **What to look for:** Leftover bracketed instructional text, undeleted underlines, orphaned optional clauses. A signature line alone on a page. Paper filings printed one side only. A return-receipt postcard addressed and enclosed if mailing.
- **Why it matters:** Template artifacts invite objections. A missing postcard leaves no independent proof of what was deposited.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.52; MPEP 502–503.

### `I48` Confirm the correct PTO destination and correspondence routing for each paper
- **Check:** Verify that papers requiring a special Mail Stop are addressed to it and that the correspondence address on the ADS is one the applicant will monitor.
- **Severity:** Quality
- **Applies to:** All applications, especially paper filings and packets containing assignments
- **What to look for:** Assignments, PCT papers, sequence-listing CRFs, and publication correspondence each have their own Mail Stop as the first address line. Confirm the current list on uspto.gov — it changes. Nothing excluded from the central fax number is being faxed. The ADS correspondence address must be live: the filing receipt (and the foreign-filing license) go there.
- **Why it matters:** Misrouted papers are delayed or lost. An unmonitored correspondence address means a missed Office Action and abandonment.
- **Primary source:** MPEP 501; current USPTO mailing-and-mail-stop page.
