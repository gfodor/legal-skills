# Part H — Drawings

22 items (3 Fatal).

Grounded in 35 U.S.C. § 113; 37 CFR 1.81–1.85; MPEP 608.02. Color, photo, and paper-size rules move — confirm the current rule text and OPAP checklist before filing.

## Drawing–specification–claim consistency

### `H01` Confirm drawings exist and show every feature recited in the claims
- **Check:** Verify that a drawing is filed wherever one is needed to understand the invention, and that every element and limitation recited in the claims is actually depicted in at least one figure.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Walk each claim element to a figure and numeral. Late-added claim elements (a spring, a controller, a second aperture) are the usual misses. Flowcharts count for process cases. **This agent must see the sheets as images.**
- **Why it matters:** A claimed limitation with no drawing support draws an objection. Because new matter cannot be added, the missing structure may not be curable.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 113; 37 CFR 1.81, 1.83; MPEP 608.02.

### `H02` Verify the drawings are complete enough to stand alone
- **Check:** Verify that the drawings contain everything technically necessary to disclose the invention, on the assumption that nothing can be added to them after the filing date.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Informal execution is curable; missing technical content is not. Assemblies drawn as an undifferentiated blob, and internal structure described in the text but never illustrated, fail. A reader who has not read the text should still understand the invention from the sheets.
- **Why it matters:** Omitted technical content is new matter. The only alternative is refiling and losing the original date.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. §§ 113, 132; 37 CFR 1.81; MPEP 608.04.

### `H03` Verify every reference numeral is mentioned in the specification and used consistently
- **Check:** Verify that each reference numeral on the sheets appears in the specification, that the same part carries the same numeral in every figure, and that no numeral designates two different parts.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** A two-column reconciliation both ways. Primed and letter-suffixed numbers count as different numbers. Recycled numbers after a revision are a common defect.
- **Why it matters:** Unmentioned or duplicated numerals are on the OPAP defect list and can undermine claim support.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.84(p); MPEP 608.02(e).

### `H04` Verify graphic symbols and labeled boxes are defined in the specification
- **Check:** Verify that every graphical symbol, encircled letter, or labeled rectangular box standing in for a conventional component is expressly explained in the specification.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** utility
- **What to look for:** An encircled "M," a box labeled "CPU," IEEE symbols, logic symbols. Each needs a definitional sentence. Arrows should show direction of movement where needed.
- **Why it matters:** An undefined symbol leaves a claimed conventional element unillustrated and unexplained.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.84(n), (o); MPEP 608.02.

### `H05` Verify improvement inventions separate the new portion and label prior-art figures
- **Check:** Verify that where the invention is an improvement on an existing machine, the improved portion is shown apart from the old structure and any figure depicting prior art is labeled as such.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** utility
- **What to look for:** The improvement buried inside a fully detailed host device. One undetailed prior-art figure tagged "(prior art)" plus a detailed figure of the improved region is the usual pattern.
- **Why it matters:** Failing to label prior-art figures misrepresents the contribution. Burying the improvement makes the drawing fail to show what is claimed.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.83–1.84; MPEP 608.02.

## Sheet and format requirements

### `H06` Verify every drawing sheet is one of the two permitted sizes and all sheets match
- **Check:** Verify that all drawing sheets are exactly the same size and are either U.S. letter or A4.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Mixed letter and A4 from combining CAD and scans. Undersized or oversized paper. Drawing sheets need not match the specification size, but they must match each other. A4 is safer if foreign filing is contemplated.
- **Why it matters:** Wrong size is an express OPAP ground for replacement drawings.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.84(f); MPEP 608.02.

### `H07` Verify the margins are clear of all lines and writing
- **Check:** Verify that nothing is drawn or written in the margins, that no margin border lines are present, and that crosshairs mark two diagonally opposite margin corners.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** CAD exports scaled to the full page. A drawn rectangle framing the sight. Confirm current 37 CFR 1.84(g) margin dimensions; when uncertain, use a wider margin.
- **Why it matters:** Incorrect margins are an OPAP defect, and content in the margin may be cropped at publication.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.84(g); MPEP 608.02.

### `H08` Verify sheet physical integrity, paper quality, single-sided use, and identification
- **Check:** Verify the sheets are clean, hole-free, unwrinkled, printed on one side only on suitable paper, and carry identifying information as currently required.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Punch holes, fold creases, gray copier lines, printing on the reverse. For paper filings, confirm current identification-on-back practice in 37 CFR 1.84(c) and ship sheets flat.
- **Why it matters:** Physical defects produce a drawing objection. Unidentified sheets can be lost if separated from the file.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.84(a)–(e); MPEP 608.02.

## Line work, shading, and sectioning

### `H09` Verify all lines are crisp, black, instrument-made, and free of solid black areas
- **Check:** Verify every line is sharp, uniformly dense, and black, drawn with drafting tools or CAD rather than freehand, with no solid black fills.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Pale gray lines, jagged bitmapped slants, crowded lines that will merge on reduction, filled black regions used as shading.
- **Why it matters:** Lines too light to reproduce are an express OPAP ground for replacement drawings.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.84(a), (l); MPEP 608.02.

### `H10` Verify each line type carries its correct meaning
- **Check:** Verify that solid, hidden, projection, and phantom lines are used only for their defined purposes, that broken lines never depict actual parts of the invention, and that no construction, center, or figure-connecting lines appear.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Dashes used for a lightly emphasized real component. Construction leftovers. Figures joined by a line. Movable parts: main position in full lines, secondary in phantom, or a separate view.
- **Why it matters:** Misused line types change what the drawing is understood to disclose.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.84(h), (n); MPEP 608.02.

### `H11` Verify sectioned parts are hatched correctly and shading follows a consistent light convention
- **Check:** Verify every part shown in section is filled with adequately spaced slanted parallel hatching, and that any surface or edge shading is consistent (conventionally light from the upper left).
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Unhatched or solid-filled sections. Crisscross hatching. Inconsistent shade directions. Shading is mandatory in design applications and optional in utility applications.
- **Why it matters:** Hatching and shading defects are on the frequent-objection list.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.84(h), (m); MPEP 608.02; 37 CFR 1.152 (design).

### `H12` Verify the scale and lettering survive two-thirds reduction
- **Check:** Verify each figure is drawn at a scale large enough that no detail is crowded when the sheet is reduced for reproduction, and that all lettering is legible at that reduction.
- **Severity:** Quality
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Print or view at two-thirds scale. Touching lines, numerals that merge into hatching. Use a larger-scale detail view rather than crowding.
- **Why it matters:** Crowded work becomes illegible in the printed patent and can obscure the detail a claim depends on.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.84(k), (p); MPEP 608.02.

## Views, figures, and orientation

### `H13` Verify enough views of the right kinds
- **Check:** Verify the drawings include as many views as are needed, prefer perspective over unexplained engineering views, bracket exploded parts, show modifications in separate figures, and include one comprehensive view suitable for the Official Gazette.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** utility
- **What to look for:** A single elevation of a three-dimensional mechanism. Alternative embodiments overlaid in broken lines. Identify the Gazette figure.
- **Why it matters:** Too few or poorly chosen views leave claimed structure unillustrated.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.83–1.84; MPEP 608.02.

### `H14` Verify sectional and enlarged views are designated on the parent figure
- **Check:** Verify every sectional view has its cutting plane marked on the general figure with a broken line, numerals matching the sectional figure number, and arrows showing the viewing direction, and that every enlarged view's area is circled and numbered on the parent figure.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** utility
- **What to look for:** A cross-section figure with no section line on the parent. Inconsistent arrow labels.
- **Why it matters:** Undesignated sections leave the examiner unable to relate the views.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.84(h); MPEP 608.02.

### `H15` Verify figures are separately and consecutively numbered, unconnected, and assemblable
- **Check:** Verify every view carries its own consecutive figure number, that no figure touches or is nested inside another, and that any drawing spanning multiple sheets can be assembled without concealing parts.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Multiple views sharing one caption. Unlabeled insets. A large machine whose sheet edges do not register.
- **Why it matters:** Unnumbered or connected figures are named OPAP defects.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.84(u); MPEP 608.02.

### `H16` Verify sheet orientation, lettering orientation, and sheet numbering
- **Check:** Verify all views on a sheet share one orientation, that lettering follows that orientation, that the sheet number and total appear at the top, and that figure legends are correctly placed.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Mixed portrait and landscape on one sheet. Sheet numbers that move with landscape rotation. Confirm current 37 CFR 1.84(i) and (t) placement.
- **Why it matters:** Inconsistent orientation and missing sheet numbers produce objections and misassembly risk.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.84(i), (t); MPEP 608.02.

## Reference numerals and lead lines

### `H17` Verify reference numerals are plain, large enough, uncircled, and clear of hatching
- **Check:** Verify every reference numeral is plainly formed, at least the current minimum height, not encircled, not crossing other lines, and not sitting on hatched surfaces unless unavoidable.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** CAD defaults smaller than 1/8 inch / 0.32 cm. Circled numbers borrowed from engineering practice. Where a numeral must sit on hatching, underline it in a cleared blank.
- **Why it matters:** Unreadable numerals break the link between the specification and the illustrated structure.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.84(p); MPEP 608.02.

### `H18` Verify every reference numeral has a lead line; arrowheads only for assemblies
- **Check:** Verify each reference numeral is connected by a lead line to the feature it designates, with no arrowhead on lead lines pointing to individual parts.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Floating numbers. Arrowheads on single-part lead lines. Assembly references may use an arrowhead or underlining. Lead lines to phantom parts should themselves be broken.
- **Why it matters:** A missing lead line is an express OPAP ground for a Notice requiring replacement drawings.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.84(q); MPEP 608.02.

## Descriptive and extraneous matter

### `H19` Verify the sheets carry no descriptive or extraneous matter beyond permitted legends
- **Check:** Verify each figure is captioned with its figure number alone, that any descriptive legends appear only where the rules permit, and that nothing unrelated to the claimed invention appears on the sheets.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** "Fig. 1 — Apparatus in Ready State." Company logos, confidentiality notices, dimension tables, drafter stamps. Use-demonstrating matter (a hand on a grip) is permitted. Text must be in English and not excessive.
- **Why it matters:** Extraneous matter and excess or non-English text are OPAP defects.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.84(o), (p), (u); MPEP 608.02.

## Photographs and color

### `H20` Verify color drawings or photos are accompanied by the petition, fee, and specification statement
- **Check:** Verify that if any drawing or photo is in color, the filing includes the current petition, fee, required specification statement, and the correct number of sets or EFS document code.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** photographs, colour drawings
- **What to look for:** Color is permitted only when necessary. Confirm current 37 CFR 1.84(a)(2) petition, fee, and the first-paragraph statement in the brief description of the drawings. Color is not acceptable in a PCT international application.
- **Why it matters:** Color filed without the required petition is an express OPAP ground for replacement drawings.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.84(a)(2); MPEP 608.02; PCT Rule 11.

### `H21` Verify photographs are used only where necessary
- **Check:** Verify that no photograph is used where a line drawing would suffice, that any black-and-white photo is supported by a petition explaining its necessity, and that all photos reproduce every detail and illustrate all features of the invention.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** photographs
- **What to look for:** The accepted justification is fine detail a drawing cannot carry (for example a photomicrograph). Soft focus, poor lighting, and shadowed claimed features fail. Prefer tracing or CAD conversion.
- **Why it matters:** A photo used where a figure would suffice is an OPAP defect. A photo that hides a claimed feature leaves that feature unillustrated with no way to add it later.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.84(b); MPEP 608.02.

## Overall drawing execution

### `H22` Verify the drawing set as a whole is well executed
- **Check:** Verify the drawings are of a standard the auditor would be willing to put in front of an examiner or a judge, rather than merely the minimum that might slip through.
- **Severity:** Quality
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Inconsistent style, weight, and detail. One polished CAD figure beside three scanned pencil sketches. Observed leniency in some issued patents is not a license — the examiner still has discretion.
- **Why it matters:** Sloppy drawings make a lasting negative impression and invite objections, replacement-drawing cycles, and delay.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.84; MPEP 608.02; 35 U.S.C. § 113.
