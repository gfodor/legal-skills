# Part B — Prior-art search adequacy

22 items (1 Fatal).

Grounded in 35 U.S.C. §§ 102–103, 37 CFR 1.56 and 1.97–1.98, MPEP 900 / 2100 / 2001, and current USPTO search resources. A search is not legally required to file, but an uninformed filing is how claims get written broader than the art allows.

## Was a search done at all

### `B01` Confirm a patentability search preceded the decision to file
- **Check:** Verify that a documented patentability search of the invention exists and was completed before the decision to file this draft was made.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** A search report, worksheets, saved queries, or annotated hit lists dated before claim lock. A marketing survey, an FTO opinion on in-force claims, or a single web query is not a patentability search. A search run after the claims were frozen, and never used to revise them, is the same defect.
- **Why it matters:** Filing without a search risks an immediate § 102/§ 103 rejection on art the drafter never saw, and wasted fees on claims the art does not permit.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. §§ 102–103; MPEP 904 (Office search practice as the coverage standard to emulate); 37 CFR 1.56.

### `B02` Test any decision to skip the search against documented coverage
- **Check:** If no search was made, verify the file records a reason that the relevant art is already known to the applicant with documented coverage — for example a field the applicant actually monitors, or an improvement already covered by an earlier search of the same concept.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Vague industry tenure is not coverage. Test whether the applicant actually tracks the journals, patents, and products of this subclass. For an "already searched" improvement, locate the earlier search and confirm its classes and keywords reach the new feature. Fast-moving fields make a skip harder to justify, not easier.
- **Why it matters:** A skipped search rationalized after the fact leaves the draft exposed to art the applicant assumed away.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. §§ 102–103; MPEP 904; 37 CFR 1.56 (duty attaches to what the applicant actually knows).

## Search scope and sources

### `B03` Verify the searcher was given every embodiment and the broad concept
- **Check:** Confirm the search request (or the DIY search plan) disclosed all embodiments, variations, and ramifications of the invention, and asked for the broad concept as well as the specific form.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Compare the search request against the specification. Embodiments added after the request went out are unsearched. A request that describes only the commercial prototype, or a sketch with no written description, searched a different invention than the one being claimed.
- **Why it matters:** Unsearched embodiments are drafted blind. Claims covering them can be knocked out by references a complete request would have surfaced.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 112(a) (the claimed embodiments must be enabled — and should be searched); MPEP 904.02.

### `B04` Verify the search record states exactly what was searched
- **Check:** Confirm the search record identifies the classes, subclasses, keywords, and databases actually searched, plus any examiner consulted, and a restatement of the invention as searched.
- **Severity:** Quality
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** A competent record has the invention as understood by the searcher, the hit list, a discussion of close references, and the classes/keywords used. A bare list of patent numbers is unauditable. If the searcher's restatement omits a claimed feature, that feature was not searched.
- **Why it matters:** Without a coverage record there is no way to know which classes were never opened.
- **Primary source:** MPEP 719.05, 904.02 (what a documented search contains).

### `B05` Confirm pre-grant published applications were searched, not just issued patents
- **Check:** Verify the search covered U.S. pre-grant application publications in the same classes and subclasses as the issued-patent search.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** A hit list of only issued patent numbers is the tell that PGPub was never run. Abandoned published applications remain prior art. Applications younger than 18 months, or under a nonpublication request, remain invisible — record that residual limit.
- **Why it matters:** A published application is § 102 art the examiner will find even if it never issued.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 122(b); 35 U.S.C. § 102(a)(2); MPEP 1120, 2154.

### `B06` Confirm non-patent literature and foreign documents were covered
- **Check:** Verify the search extended beyond the U.S. patent database to non-patent literature and to foreign patent documents where the technology warrants it.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Journals, standards, manuals, theses, archived websites, and Espacenet / PATENTSCOPE / national-office hits. USPTO Patent Public Search does not replace a foreign or NPL pass. Date-verify every NPL item used as art.
- **Why it matters:** An article, standard, or foreign patent anticipates as completely as a U.S. patent.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 102(a)(1); MPEP 2126–2128; MPEP 904.02(c).

### `B07` Grade the search by its source and discount what cannot be searched
- **Check:** Verify that the confidence placed in the search matches the source used, and that the draft is not written as if the search were exhaustive.
- **Severity:** Quality
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Unpublished pending applications cannot be searched. Recent filings may not be loaded. Public use and sales rarely appear in patent databases. A cheap, same-week internet search in a crowded field is not an examiner-grade search. Record those limits in the audit.
- **Why it matters:** Over-trusting a shallow search produces broad claims the later Office search will destroy.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 102(a)(2) (secret art); MPEP 904.

## Classification and keyword coverage

### `B08` Confirm both a classification search and a keyword search were run
- **Check:** Verify the search record shows both a CPC classification search and a keyword/Boolean search, each used to check the other.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Keyword-only and classification-only records have opposite blind spots: wrong vocabulary, or wrong class. Overlap — the keyword pass re-finding classification hits — is the best coverage sign. New classes surfaced by keywords should then be searched.
- **Why it matters:** Either method alone can miss the closest reference, which then appears for the first time in an Office Action.
- **Primary source:** MPEP 904.02; CPC Scheme and Definitions (uspto.gov).

### `B09` Confirm all plausible CPC codes, including analogous fields, were checked
- **Check:** Verify the classification search covered more than one CPC code and reached analogous arts, not just the single most obvious subclass.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** A worksheet with exactly one subclass is almost always under-searched. Follow informative references in the CPC definitions. Search analogous apparatus that solves the same mechanical or data problem in another field.
- **Why it matters:** The closest reference often sits one subclass away.
- **Primary source:** MPEP 904.02(a); CPC Definitions.

### `B10` Confirm backward and forward citation chaining from the closest references
- **Check:** Verify that for each closely relevant reference the searcher followed its cited art backward and its citing art forward, and mined its field of search for further classes.
- **Severity:** Quality
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Close hits used as seeds, not endpoints. Backward chaining reaches examiner-vetted art; forward chaining catches recent publications. Pre-1950 U.S. patents often lack a modern cited-references list — classification must carry older art.
- **Why it matters:** Skipping the citation tree leaves the art an examiner is most likely to cite unexamined.
- **Primary source:** MPEP 904.02; USPTO Patent Public Search citation tools.

### `B11` Check database date coverage against the age of the technology
- **Check:** Verify that the databases searched actually reach back far enough to cover the era in which prior art for this technology would have been published.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications; especially low-tech / long-established fields
- **What to look for:** Full-text U.S. keyword search is reliable only for modern text. Older mechanical and household art requires classification search back to the early patent corpus. If the basic idea could predate full-text coverage, a keyword-only search is incomplete.
- **Why it matters:** Century-old art anticipates modern claims. An examiner running classification will find the patent the keyword search never returned.
- **Primary source:** USPTO Patent Public Search coverage notes; MPEP 904.02.

### `B12` Check the keyword set for synonyms, jargon, and pre-adoption terminology
- **Check:** Verify the recorded search terms include alternative words, technical jargon equivalents, wildcards, and the terminology that would have been used before the technology acquired its modern name.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Claim terms that never appear in the search record. Two or three obvious nouns with no synonyms. Patents often use legal or functional language instead of the marketplace name. Early foundational patents may predate the common name entirely.
- **Why it matters:** A vocabulary mismatch returns a clean result on art that plainly anticipates the claims.
- **Primary source:** MPEP 904.02(b) (search queries); CPC catchword index practice.

## Analysing the references found

### `B13` Confirm each reference was read as a disclosure, not as a set of claims
- **Check:** Verify that the analysis of each close reference addressed its description and drawings, and did not conclude non-relevance because the reference's claims do not cover the invention.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Notes that say "claims don't cover us." A patent is prior art for everything it enables, as of its relevant date, whether or not it claims that subject matter. Infringement of an in-force patent is a separate clearance question the Office does not examine.
- **Why it matters:** Dismissing a reference on its claims leaves an anticipating disclosure unaccounted for.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 102; MPEP 2121, 2131; MPEP 2103 (Office does not determine infringement).

### `B14` Verify the effective date and document type of every cited reference
- **Check:** Confirm each reference's date and kind were correctly determined, including expired patents, patents effective as of their filing or publication date, and pre-grant publications distinguished from granted patents.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Treating an expired patent as irrelevant; using only the issue date when the AIA filing date controls; confusing a PGPub with a patent. Record kind codes, publication dates, and effective prior-art dates.
- **Why it matters:** A reference wrongly written off as "expired" or "too late" stays out of claim drafting and out of the IDS.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 102(a), (d); MPEP 2152, 2154; WIPO ST.16 kind codes.

### `B15` Confirm close references were studied in full
- **Check:** Verify that every reference retained as relevant was obtained in full and analysed, rather than judged from an abstract page, a single figure, or a title.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Screening from abstracts is fine for elimination. Stopping there is not. A figure not shown on the abstract page may be the anticipating embodiment. Write a short technical summary of each retained reference.
- **Why it matters:** A reference dismissed on a partial view is the one the examiner cites after the fee is paid.
- **Primary source:** MPEP 2121, 2131 (the whole reference is available as a disclosure).

### `B16` Confirm a written patentability analysis exists, run in the correct direction
- **Check:** Verify the file contains the applicant's own novelty and nonobviousness analysis against the closest references, listing the differences the invention has over the art — not the differences the art has over the invention — and addressing combinations of references.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** The required list is what the claim has that no one reference shows. Then ask whether those differences would have been obvious, including combinations. A searcher's silence is not clearance. Lay searchers often cannot give a patentability opinion; their omission means nothing.
- **Why it matters:** Without this analysis the drafter cannot know which features must appear in the independent claims.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. §§ 102–103; MPEP 2131, 2141.

## Feeding the search back into the draft

### `B17` Confirm the specification is aimed at the novel features and the closest art's deficiencies
- **Check:** Verify the draft specification recites and stresses the features found novel in the search, and explains their advantages against the deficiencies of the closest references.
- **Severity:** Quality
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Generic background that never engages the closest reference. A passing draft identifies what the closest art fails to do and directs the description and quantitative results at that gap. Every feature relied on for novelty must appear in the written description.
- **Why it matters:** Missing support cannot be added later without new matter. The first Office Action will demand a distinction the specification never prepared.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 112(a); MPEP 608.01, 2163.

### `B18` Confirm claim scope was set against the found art
- **Check:** Verify the independent claims recite the fewest/broadest features that both distinguish over the closest references under § 102 and supply a nonobvious difference under § 103 — and that the resulting scope is still worth filing on.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Claims left at pre-search breadth, or narrowed further than the art requires. Walk the independent claim: add limitations only until novelty and nonobviousness are facially present. If many very specific features are required, a competitor can avoid the claim easily — question whether filing is worth it.
- **Why it matters:** Claims broader than the art supports are invalid or unallowable. Claims narrower than necessary give away scope.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. §§ 102, 103, 112; MPEP 2173.05.

### `B19` Confirm any teach-away reference found is preserved and used
- **Check:** Verify that references found in the search which teach away from the invention are identified in the file and reflected in the draft's discussion of unexpected results.
- **Severity:** Quality
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** References stating the approach will not work, should not be used, or failed. These are often discarded as "not relevant" because they do not show the invention. They are often the strongest § 103 evidence.
- **Why it matters:** Losing a teach-away forfeits the best available argument against an obviousness rejection.
- **Primary source:** MPEP 2145 (teaching away); *In re Gurley*.

### `B20` Confirm every material reference is queued for the Information Disclosure Statement
- **Check:** Verify that every relevant reference the search produced is on a list to be recorded in the PTO's file of this application, and that the search did not conclude with zero references.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Reconcile the search record against the IDS working list item by item. References judged "not close enough" are the ones most often dropped — that judgment is not the applicant's alone under 37 CFR 1.56. A search that ended with no references is not finished; cite the closest art even if it is not anticipating.
- **Why it matters:** A material reference known to the applicant and never disclosed can support inequitable-conduct unenforceability. Disclosing it lets the claims be tailored around it.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.56, 1.97, 1.98; MPEP 2001, 609; *Therasense v. Becton, Dickinson*.

### `B21` Confirm named-only components are documented in the found art or described
- **Check:** Verify that each component the specification passes over by its well-known name — rather than describing and drawing it — is in fact already described in a prior-art publication located by the search.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications
- **What to look for:** Parts mentioned but not illustrated or explained, especially outside the inventor's expertise. The license to name rather than describe depends on the component being known. Proprietary or novel parts must be drawn and described.
- **Why it matters:** A component neither described nor documented in the art is an enablement hole that cannot be cured after filing.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 112(a); MPEP 2164.01.

### `B22` Discount and supplement the search for software and business-method inventions
- **Check:** For a software or business-method draft, verify the search went beyond the PTO patent database and that the claims are not resting on the absence of hits in it.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** software / business method
- **What to look for:** Software and business methods are poorly and inconsistently classified. Compensate with NPL, product manuals, archived sites, source repositories, and specialist databases. Treat "nothing found in USPTO search" as the weakest form of clearance in this field.
- **Why it matters:** These claims are disproportionately invalidated by art that never appeared in the original patent search.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. §§ 102–103; MPEP 2106 (eligibility is separate from search quality); MPEP 904.02.
