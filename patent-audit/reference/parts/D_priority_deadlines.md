# Part D — Priority, deadlines, and foreign rights

46 items (16 Fatal).

Grounded in 35 U.S.C. §§ 111, 119–121, 154, 172, 184–186, 251; 37 CFR 1.7, 1.53, 1.55, 1.78, 1.213; MPEP 200–270, 1400; Paris Convention; PCT. Recompute every date against the filing receipt and current 37 CFR 1.7 rollover.

## Auditing a draft provisional — disclosure sufficiency

### `D01` Verify the provisional teaches how to make and use the invention
- **Check:** The provisional's description, standing alone, clearly teaches a person skilled in the field how to make and use the invention.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** PPA only
- **What to look for:** A provisional that recites purpose and benefits but never gives structure, materials, connections, or an operating sequence. A slide deck or paper dropped in as a provisional often describes results without construction. Every later-claimed embodiment must be enabled here if its date will be needed.
- **Why it matters:** A provisional that does not satisfy 35 U.S.C. § 112(a) cannot support the later nonprovisional. Intervening art then defeats those claims.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. §§ 111(b), 119(e), 112(a); *New Railhead Mfg. v. Vermeer*; MPEP 211.05.

### `D02` Verify the best mode is disclosed
- **Check:** If the inventor currently prefers one version or mode of the invention, that preferred version is actually disclosed in the provisional (and in the regular application).
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** PPA only; also the regular application
- **What to look for:** A generic write-up while the working configuration — specific material, circuit, or parameters — is held back. AIA removed best-mode invalidity as a defense, but best mode remains a filing requirement and withholding it can still destroy priority support and enablement.
- **Why it matters:** Subject matter not disclosed gets no provisional date. Withheld technical detail also risks a § 112(a) hole in the nonprovisional.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 112(a); 35 U.S.C. § 282(b)(3)(A); MPEP 2165.

### `D03` Verify every embodiment you might later claim is described
- **Check:** The provisional describes all embodiments, variations, and ramifications the applicant might want to claim in the later regular application.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** PPA only
- **What to look for:** A provisional covering only the prototype, with variants living in notebooks. A one-line "other variations are possible" is not a description. Uncertain variants still belong in the provisional if they may later be claimed.
- **Why it matters:** Anything not disclosed gets no benefit of the provisional date.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 119(e); MPEP 211.05, 2163.

### `D04` Verify drawings are present, understandable, and permanent
- **Check:** Drawings are included wherever they are needed to understand how to make and use the invention, and both drawings and text are in permanent form.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** PPA only
- **What to look for:** Informal figures are acceptable in a provisional, but missing, illegible, or pencil-only figures that will not survive as a permanent record undermine enablement. Every figure referenced in the text should exist, and every essential part should be findable.
- **Why it matters:** Missing drawings can destroy the make-and-use disclosure that later claims need.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 111(b); 37 CFR 1.53(c); MPEP 601.01(b).

### `D05` Verify the provisional is written to regular-application disclosure quality
- **Check:** The provisional follows the format and quality of a regular application's specification closely enough that a later benefit claim will be defensible.
- **Severity:** Quality
- **Applies to:** PPA only
- **What to look for:** Optional but useful sections (background, figure descriptions, summary, detailed description, abstract) present and technically complete. Undefined jargon and assumed-expert openings make later support fights harder.
- **Why it matters:** The closer the provisional is to the later specification, the more confidently its date can be claimed.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 119(e); MPEP 211.05, 608.01.

### `D06` Verify at least one claim is included in the provisional
- **Check:** The provisional contains at least one claim, even though claims are not required for a U.S. provisional.
- **Severity:** Quality
- **Applies to:** PPA only; important where foreign priority is contemplated
- **What to look for:** Drafting a claim is a coverage test for the description. Some foreign offices expect a claim in the priority document. Every claim limitation must already be described.
- **Why it matters:** A claimless priority document can be challenged abroad, and a claim-drafting exercise exposes missing description before filing.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 111(b) (claims optional); Paris Convention practice; MPEP 201.04.

## Provisional formal papers, fees, and eligibility

### `D07` Verify all required provisional papers are present and complete
- **Check:** The filing package contains every required part — description, drawings if needed, cover sheet, fee, entity certification if claimed, and ADS if used.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** PPA only
- **What to look for:** Inventory against 37 CFR 1.53(c). A specification with no cover sheet or missing fee is incomplete. Keep a complete copy of everything filed.
- **Why it matters:** An incomplete provisional cannot be relied on for its filing date by the later nonprovisional.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 111(b); 37 CFR 1.51, 1.53(c); MPEP 601.01.

### `D08` Verify the fee is correct and the entity-status claim is truthful
- **Check:** The proper filing fee accompanies the provisional and any small-entity or micro-entity status asserted is actually supported for every named inventor.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** PPA only; same discipline applies to the regular application
- **What to look for:** Test small-entity (37 CFR 1.27) and micro-entity (37 CFR 1.29) per inventor, including prior-application counts and income limits. An assignment obligation to a disqualifying entity kills the discount. Re-price against the current fee schedule.
- **Why it matters:** Missing the fee can leave the provisional incomplete. A false entity certification is a fraud on the Office.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.16, 1.27, 1.29; 35 U.S.C. § 41.

### `D09` Verify every contributor to the inventive subject matter is named
- **Check:** All persons who contributed to the inventive subject matter disclosed in the provisional are named as inventors on it.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** PPA only
- **What to look for:** Map each inventive concept to the person who conceived it. A solo-named provisional that discloses a colleague's variation breaks the inventorship chain the later benefit claim needs.
- **Why it matters:** Omitting a contributor undermines both the priority claim and later proof of who invented what.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. §§ 111(b), 116, 119(e); MPEP 210.

### `D10` Verify the provisional is eligible subject matter and claims no earlier priority
- **Check:** The provisional is not directed to a design, and it contains no attempt to claim the benefit of any earlier domestic or foreign application.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** PPA only
- **What to look for:** 35 U.S.C. § 172 makes provisionals unavailable for designs. A provisional cannot claim benefit of an earlier application (37 CFR 1.78). ADS templates copied from a nonprovisional often fill those sections by mistake.
- **Why it matters:** An ineligible or improperly formatted provisional gives no effective filing date.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. §§ 111(b), 172; 37 CFR 1.78; MPEP 201.04.

## Claiming priority from a provisional in a regular application

### `D11` Verify every regular-application claim is supported by the provisional
- **Check:** Each claim of the draft regular application is fully described — make-and-use level — in the provisional whose date is being claimed.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** Applications claiming PPA priority
- **What to look for:** Limitation-by-limitation support map. Ranges, materials, and "wherein" clauses added during claim drafting are the usual orphans. A genus claim needs more than one undisclosed species in the provisional.
- **Why it matters:** Unsupported claims silently lose the provisional date while appearing to have one.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 119(e); *Dynamic Drinkware*; MPEP 211.05, 2163.

### `D12` Verify post-provisional changes are properly captured
- **Check:** Any change or improvement made after the provisional was filed is either covered by a subsequent provisional or carried into the regular application with no expectation of the earlier date.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** Applications claiming PPA priority
- **What to look for:** Diff the two texts. A provisional cannot be amended. Added subject matter gets only the later date. Make that split explicit in the priority strategy.
- **Why it matters:** Applicants who believe a provisional can be "updated" build claims on a date they do not have.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. §§ 111(b), 119(e); 37 CFR 1.53(c); MPEP 201.04.

### `D13` Verify inventor overlap between the provisional and the regular application
- **Check:** At least one inventor named in the provisional is also named in the regular application, and that inventor's invention is both claimed in the regular application and fully disclosed in the provisional.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** Applications claiming PPA priority
- **What to look for:** Compare inventor lists. Inventorship often changes between filings. Both name overlap and subject-matter overlap are required.
- **Why it matters:** Without the required overlap the regular application cannot claim the provisional date at all.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 119(e); 37 CFR 1.78; MPEP 211.02.

### `D14` Verify the regular application actually claims the benefit of the provisional
- **Check:** The draft regular application contains an express reference claiming the benefit of the provisional's filing date.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** Applications claiming PPA priority
- **What to look for:** The claim must appear in the ADS (37 CFR 1.78) and should also appear in a cross-reference paragraph. Confirm serial number and date are correct. Silence means the Office will disregard the provisional.
- **Why it matters:** A timely, well-supported application that never refers to its provisional gets no earlier date.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 119(e); 37 CFR 1.78; MPEP 211.02.

### `D15` Verify each provisional relied on was filed within the preceding year
- **Check:** Where the regular application claims benefit of more than one provisional, every one of them was filed within the year preceding the regular application's filing date, and each invention claimed is traced to a provisional that discloses it.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** Applications claiming priority from multiple PPAs
- **What to look for:** A table of each provisional, its date, days remaining, and the claims it supports. The oldest one is usually the one that has quietly expired.
- **Why it matters:** A provisional outside the one-year window contributes nothing.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 119(e); 37 CFR 1.78.

## The 12-month deadline, foreign rights, and publication

### `D16` Verify the regular application will be filed within one year of the provisional
- **Check:** The regular application's projected filing date falls within twelve months of the provisional's filing date, with margin.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** Applications claiming PPA priority
- **What to look for:** Use the filing-receipt date, not the date papers were prepared. Calendar a decision point well before the anniversary. 37 CFR 1.7 rollover applies; foreign offices may not honor a U.S. weekend extension.
- **Why it matters:** If no regular application is filed within the year, the provisional is abandoned and its date can never be claimed.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 119(e); 37 CFR 1.7; MPEP 211.01(a).

### `D17` Verify the foreign-filing decision is made inside the provisional year
- **Check:** Any foreign or PCT filing that is to claim the provisional's priority is scheduled within one year of the provisional's filing date, and non-Convention countries are addressed before any publication or public sale.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** Applications claiming PPA priority where foreign rights are wanted
- **What to look for:** The Convention year runs from the first filing — the provisional — not from the later U.S. nonprovisional. Missing that fact is the usual trap.
- **Why it matters:** Missing the year forfeits foreign priority. Publishing before filing in an absolute-novelty country forfeits rights there outright.
- **Primary source:** Paris Convention Art. 4; 35 U.S.C. § 119; PCT Art. 8.

### `D18` Verify the nonpublication-request decision is made at filing
- **Check:** The applicant has consciously decided whether to file a Nonpublication Request with the regular application, understanding that publication runs from the earliest claimed filing date.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** Applications claiming PPA priority; all applications where trade secret status matters
- **What to look for:** With a provisional in the chain, 18-month publication is measured from the provisional date (35 U.S.C. § 122(b)). An NPR can be filed only at the time of filing the nonprovisional and is inconsistent with foreign filing.
- **Why it matters:** Missing the NPR publishes the application earlier than expected and ends trade-secret status in the disclosed subject matter.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 122(b); 37 CFR 1.213; MPEP 1122.

## Inventorship and conception records

### `D19` Verify a signed, dated, and witnessed record of conception exists
- **Check:** There is a written record of conception, in permanent form, signed and dated by the inventor and corroborated by witnesses who understood it.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** All applications, especially where derivation or disputed inventorship is a live risk
- **What to look for:** Under first-inventor-to-file, conception date rarely wins a race — filing does. Conception records still matter for derivation (35 U.S.C. § 135), inventorship, and employment disputes. Unsigned or unwitnessed write-ups, or records created only now and backdated, fail.
- **Why it matters:** Without this record the inventor may be unable to prove who invented what when a derivation or ownership fight starts.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. §§ 116, 135; MPEP 2138 (pre-AIA interference context) and current derivation practice.

### `D20` Verify the witnesses are competent, impartial, and not potential co-inventors
- **Check:** Each witness on the conception or build-and-test record is technically able to understand the invention, is impartial, and actually read and understood the subject matter.
- **Severity:** Quality
- **Applies to:** All applications relying on witnessed records
- **What to look for:** Close relatives, collaborators who may be co-inventors, the prosecuting attorney, and signature-only notaries are weak corroboration. Two knowledgeable, unrelated witnesses are stronger than one.
- **Why it matters:** Weak witnesses give the record little weight when derivation or inventorship is contested.
- **Primary source:** corroboration doctrine (*Mahurkar*, *Price v. Symsek*); 35 U.S.C. § 135.

### `D21` Verify the conception record captures variants and claimed features
- **Check:** The conception record covers purpose, description, sketch, variants, asserted novel features, closest known art, and advantages — not just the core embodiment.
- **Severity:** Quality
- **Applies to:** All applications; conception and invention-disclosure records
- **What to look for:** Omitted variants that a witness later supplies can create a co-inventorship claim. The record should let a skilled person understand what was conceived on that date.
- **Why it matters:** A thin record invites inventorship claims and fails as proof of what was conceived.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. §§ 116, 135.

### `D22` Reject sealed-envelope and electronic-only records as sole proof
- **Check:** The file does not rely on a mailed-to-self sealed envelope or on purely electronic records to establish conception; anything created on a computer has been printed and signed or otherwise independently corroborated.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** All applications relying on documentation of conception
- **What to look for:** "Poor man's patent" envelopes are essentially worthless. Undated, uncorroborated files in a cloud folder are weak. Prefer signed, witnessed, hash-dated, or independently archived records.
- **Why it matters:** Electronic-only or self-mailed records rarely survive a contested inventorship or derivation proceeding.
- **Primary source:** corroboration doctrine; 35 U.S.C. § 135.

## Confidentiality before filing

### `D23` Verify pre-filing disclosures were made under a signed NDA
- **Check:** Every person or organization shown the invention before filing — shops, consultants, prospective partners — signed a nondisclosure agreement, and the transactions are documented.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** All applications, for disclosures made before the PPA or regular application was filed
- **What to look for:** Match each recipient against a signed, dated NDA. Vendor parts orders that reveal purpose, informal pitches, and undocumented prototypes are gaps. Keep a paper trail.
- **Why it matters:** Undocumented disclosures put trade-secret status and derivation proof at risk, and can start a public-use or on-sale clock.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 102(a)(1); state trade-secret law; 35 U.S.C. § 135.

## Preserving foreign rights

### `D24` Confirm no pre-filing disclosure has already killed foreign rights
- **Check:** Verify that the invention was not published, sold, or otherwise publicly disclosed before the U.S. filing date, because most foreign countries have no grace period.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** all applications where foreign rights may be wanted
- **What to look for:** Trade shows, crowdfunding, papers, listings, videos, site launches. If disclosure already occurred, record which absolute-novelty countries are gone. Do not pay for filings that are already dead.
- **Why it matters:** Permanent loss of rights in most foreign countries, plus wasted foreign fees.
- **Primary source:** Paris Convention; national absolute-novelty laws; 35 U.S.C. § 119.

### `D25` File in non-Convention countries before any publication or sale
- **Check:** Verify that, if any non-Convention country is on the target list, filings there are scheduled before the invention or the application becomes publicly known.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** applications with foreign filing intent in non-Convention jurisdictions
- **What to look for:** Non-Convention filings get no Paris priority. PCT/EPO publication at 18 months will destroy novelty there if those filings are not already on file.
- **Why it matters:** Once the invention is public, filing in a non-Convention country is usually impossible.
- **Primary source:** national novelty statutes of the target countries; PCT Art. 21.

### `D26` Test the commercial case for each foreign country before committing
- **Check:** Verify that a documented commercial justification exists for every country on the foreign-filing list.
- **Severity:** Quality
- **Applies to:** applications with foreign filing intent
- **What to look for:** A named licensee, channel, or forecast per country. "It's a global product" is not a justification. Foreign filing, translation, and annuities are expensive.
- **Why it matters:** Unjustified foreign filings consume cash that will not be recouped.
- **Primary source:** business judgment; Paris/PCT cost structure (WIPO fee tables).

## Foreign-filing deadlines to diary

### `D27` Diary the one-year Paris Convention deadline at filing
- **Check:** Verify that a calendar entry exists for one year from the earliest U.S. filing date (the provisional date if a provisional was filed).
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** all applications where foreign rights may be wanted
- **What to look for:** The deadline runs from the *earliest* filing, not from the nonprovisional. Diarising one year from the nonprovisional when a provisional was filed a year earlier means the deadline has already passed. Design Convention term is six months (35 U.S.C. § 172).
- **Why it matters:** Missing the year converts later foreign filings into non-priority applications exposed to intervening art, including the applicant's own published U.S. case.
- **Primary source:** Paris Convention Art. 4; 35 U.S.C. §§ 119, 172.

### `D28` Diary the 30-month PCT national-stage deadline
- **Check:** Verify that a 30-month-from-priority calendar reminder is set for entering the national stage in each designated PCT jurisdiction, with the applicant on notice that no office will reliably remind them.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** applications with PCT filing intent
- **What to look for:** Compute from the priority date, not the PCT filing date. Some states allow 31 months; verify per jurisdiction. Translations and local agents need lead time.
- **Why it matters:** Missing national-stage entry abandons the foreign rights the PCT filing was bought to preserve.
- **Primary source:** PCT Art. 22, 39; 37 CFR 1.495; national PCT implementing laws.

### `D29` Set the decision point months before each deadline
- **Check:** Verify that the foreign-filing *decision* is diarised two to three months ahead of the legal deadline, not on the deadline itself.
- **Severity:** Quality
- **Applies to:** applications with foreign filing intent
- **What to look for:** Agent appointment, translations, and certified copies take time. A calendar that contains only the hard deadline is the failure mode. There is usually no advantage to filing abroad immediately after the U.S. case.
- **Why it matters:** A deadline discovered with two weeks left cannot be met once translations are needed.
- **Primary source:** Paris Art. 4; PCT timeline; 37 CFR 1.55 (certified copies).

## Foreign-filing license and the six-month rule

### `D30` Do not file abroad before the foreign-filing license or six months elapse
- **Check:** Verify that no foreign filing is planned or already made before the PTO grants a foreign-filing license or six months have run from the U.S. filing date.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** all applications with foreign filing intent
- **What to look for:** Read the filing receipt for the license line. If it is absent, wait six months or petition. A foreign co-applicant's plan to file first is a red flag.
- **Why it matters:** Unlicensed early foreign filing can jeopardize the U.S. patent and violate export-control review (35 U.S.C. §§ 184–186).
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. §§ 184–186; 37 CFR 5.11–5.25; MPEP 140.

### `D31` Flag military or dual-use subject matter for secrecy-order exposure
- **Check:** Verify whether the disclosed subject matter has possible military or national-security applications, in which case no foreign-filing license will issue and a secrecy order may follow.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** applications disclosing defense, cryptographic, or dual-use technology
- **What to look for:** Cryptography, munitions, guidance, sensors, secure comms, nuclear — including commercially framed versions of the same core. Do not plan foreign filings against a license that may never arrive.
- **Why it matters:** A secrecy order stops issuance, potentially for years, and destroys any foreign schedule built on a routine license.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. §§ 181–188; 37 CFR Part 5; MPEP 120.

## Nonpublication request conflicts

### `D32` Do not file a nonpublication request if foreign filing is contemplated
- **Check:** Verify that the decision to include or omit a Nonpublication Request is consistent with the applicant's actual foreign-filing intent.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** all applications; decided at filing
- **What to look for:** An NPR is a certification that the invention will not be filed in a publishing jurisdiction. Uncertainty is itself a reason not to file an NPR. An NPR plus PCT notes in the same file is a contradiction.
- **Why it matters:** An NPR paired with an unrescinded later foreign filing causes the U.S. application to be regarded as abandoned.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 122(b); 37 CFR 1.213; MPEP 1122.

### `D33` Record the 45-day NPR rescission obligation
- **Check:** Verify that, where an NPR is being filed, the file records the duty to rescind it within 45 days of any foreign filing, including a PCT filing.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** applications filed with a Nonpublication Request
- **What to look for:** PCT filed in the USPTO is still a foreign-publishing filing for this purpose. Use PTO/SB/36. Diary the trigger next to the Convention/PCT deadline.
- **Why it matters:** Failing to rescind abandons the U.S. application. Revival requires a petition and fee.
- **Primary source:** 37 CFR 1.213(c); MPEP 1122.

## Drafting for foreign jurisdictions

### `D34` Consider claim style for the intended foreign offices
- **Check:** Verify that the claim set has been reviewed against the claiming conventions of the intended foreign jurisdictions, and that a second claim set is contemplated for a PCT filing where appropriate.
- **Severity:** Quality
- **Applies to:** applications with foreign filing intent, especially European
- **What to look for:** Spare U.S. independent claims can read as unusually narrow under central claiming. Carry enough descriptive support to draft a fuller foreign set later without new matter.
- **Why it matters:** Claims tuned only for U.S. peripheral claiming can permanently limit foreign patents.
- **Primary source:** EPC Art. 69 and Protocol; PCT Rule 6; MPEP 1800.

## Choosing the right kind of related application

### `D35` Confirm the draft is the right kind of child application
- **Check:** Verify that the draft is characterized correctly — continuation, divisional, CIP, independent, or substitute — against what it actually claims relative to the parent.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** continuations, divisionals, CIPs, independent and substitute applications
- **What to look for:** Diff the child against the parent. New disclosure means CIP, not continuation. A continuation labeled on a spec that added paragraphs will not change how claims are dated.
- **Why it matters:** A mislabeled child gets the wrong priority treatment. The applicant discovers it only after an Office Action.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. §§ 120–121, 132; MPEP 201.06–201.08.

### `D36` Verify the parent is still pending on the intended filing date
- **Check:** Verify that the parent application is still pending — neither abandoned nor issued — as of the date this child application will actually be filed.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** continuations, divisionals, CIPs
- **What to look for:** Co-pendency is required (35 U.S.C. § 120). For an allowed parent, file by the day before issue. Do not rely on ordinary first-class mail for this deadline.
- **Why it matters:** A child filed one day after the parent issues or goes abandoned has no continuity.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 120; MPEP 211.01(b).

### `D37` Flag a refiling of an abandoned case as a substitute with no priority benefit
- **Check:** Verify whether the draft is in substance a refiling of an application that was already abandoned, in which case it gets no benefit of the earlier filing date.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** refilings of abandoned applications
- **What to look for:** A duplicate of an earlier case whose parent is no longer pending, sometimes still carrying a benefit sentence. Assess intervening art, including the applicant's own published earlier application. Consider revival of the parent instead if available.
- **Why it matters:** The lost priority date exposes the application to intervening art the parent's date would have excluded.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 120; MPEP 201.09 (substitute).

### `D38` Check the divisional's claims are confined to the divided-out invention
- **Check:** Verify that a divisional's claims are directed solely to the nonelected invention and that its specification adds nothing that was not in the parent.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** divisionals
- **What to look for:** Copied parent claims to the elected invention, or editorial cleanups that slip in new matter. Compare line by line to the parent and to the restriction requirement.
- **Why it matters:** Claims outside the divided-out invention invite double patenting and can forfeit the § 121 safe harbor. New matter destroys the parent's date.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 121; MPEP 201.06, 803, 804.01.

### `D39` Verify the cross-reference and continuity data recite the complete chain
- **Check:** Verify that the CROSS-REFERENCE paragraph and the ADS continuity data name every application in the priority chain, with correct serial numbers, filing dates, and continuity types.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** continuations, divisionals, CIPs, and any application claiming domestic benefit
- **What to look for:** A chain recited only one generation deep; blank placeholders; "continuation" where new matter exists; ADS and specification that disagree. Delayed benefit claims now require a petition after two years (37 CFR 1.78).
- **Why it matters:** A defective benefit claim can cost the earlier date.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. §§ 119, 120; 37 CFR 1.78; MPEP 211.02.

### `D40` Check inventorship against the child's claims
- **Check:** Verify that the named inventors match the claims actually presented in the child application, which may differ in scope from the parent's.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** continuations, divisionals, CIPs
- **What to look for:** A CIP claiming newly added matter may need an inventor added. A narrowed continuation may need one removed. Inventorship follows the claims, not the parent's caption.
- **Why it matters:** Incorrect inventorship is a defect that follows the patent.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. §§ 116, 120; 37 CFR 1.48; MPEP 602.01.

## New matter and priority in a CIP

### `D41` Identify the CIP's new matter and mark which claims lose the parent's date
- **Check:** Verify that every element of new matter in the CIP is identified, and that each claim is mapped to the earliest date its support actually justifies.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** CIPs
- **What to look for:** Per-claim support in the *parent*. Any limitation supported only by CIP-added matter takes the CIP date, including as against the parent's own 18-month publication.
- **Why it matters:** Claims that silently drop to the later date can be invalidated by art the parent's date would have excluded.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 120; MPEP 201.08, 211.05, 2163.03.

### `D42` Screen a CIP for transition-application status before adding claims to new matter
- **Check:** Verify whether the CIP claims priority to an application filed before March 16, 2013, and if so whether any claim requires support from matter added in the CIP.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** CIPs claiming priority to pre-March 16, 2013 applications
- **What to look for:** One claim needing CIP-added support can push the entire application into AIA examination. That door does not close. Weigh a separate application instead. Check the ADS transition statement.
- **Why it matters:** AIA examination expands prior art and shrinks the grace period, irreversibly, for the whole case.
- **Primary source:** AIA first-inventor-to-file transition; 37 CFR 1.55/1.78 statements; MPEP 2159.

## Double patenting and term

### `D43` Run a double-patenting check across the whole family before filing
- **Check:** Verify that the claims of this application are patentably distinct from the claims of every other pending application and issued patent owned by the same applicant.
- **Severity:** Fatal
- **Applies to:** related-application families; any second case on the same invention
- **What to look for:** Same-invention double patenting (incurable by terminal disclaimer) versus obviousness-type. Continuations that differ from the parent only by wording are the tell. The Office will reject both co-pending cases if they are not distinct.
- **Why it matters:** Both patents can be held invalid, and pending applications will be rejected.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 101 (statutory DP); obviousness-type DP doctrine; MPEP 804.

### `D44` Decide whether a terminal disclaimer will be needed, and check common ownership
- **Check:** Verify whether the child's claims are patentably different from the parent's, and if not, that a terminal disclaimer is planned and both cases will remain commonly owned.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** continuations and CIPs where the parent will issue
- **What to look for:** A CIP dependent that adds an obvious detail over the parent. A terminal disclaimer requires common ownership to remain in place (37 CFR 1.321).
- **Why it matters:** Without a TD, both patents can be held invalid. With one, the second patent's term is cut back, and splitting ownership later can undo the disclaimer.
- **Primary source:** MPEP 804; 37 CFR 1.321.

### `D45` Confirm whether a restriction requirement actually supports a voluntary divisional
- **Check:** Verify that the divisional follows an examiner's restriction requirement, or, if voluntary, that its claims are to a clearly different invention.
- **Severity:** Serious
- **Applies to:** divisionals
- **What to look for:** Cite the restriction requirement by date and group. A voluntary "divisional" with no restriction has no § 121 safe harbor.
- **Why it matters:** A voluntary divisional that is not clearly distinct can invalidate both patents.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 121; MPEP 804.01, 803.

### `D46` Ask whether the benefit claim is worth the shortened term
- **Check:** Verify that the decision to claim benefit of the parent's filing date was made deliberately, weighing the 20-year term measured from the parent rather than from this application.
- **Severity:** Quality
- **Applies to:** continuations, divisionals, CIPs
- **What to look for:** Every continuing application expires 20 years from the parent's earliest nonprovisional date (35 U.S.C. § 154). If no intervening art requires the earlier date, filing as an independent application preserves a full term. File and prosecute promptly if benefit is claimed.
- **Why it matters:** An unnecessary benefit claim gives away years of enforceable term.
- **Primary source:** 35 U.S.C. § 154; MPEP 2701.
